# Edge-case designs in target-trial emulation and survival analysis

## TL;DR

- Sequential target trials intentionally reuse a person at multiple eligible time zeros; the analysis unit is **person–trial** (`unit_id × anchor_id`), with baseline covariates remeasured at each `t0` and inference clustered by person. [Danaei et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0962280211403603)
- A grace-period per-protocol emulation can require **clones per strategy per anchor**, artificial censoring when observed treatment conflicts with a clone's strategy, and inverse-probability-of-censoring weights (IPCW). [Hernán et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyaa057)
- Time-varying treatment/per-protocol analyses require person-period treatment, covariate, outcome, and censoring histories; a single baseline treatment or window aggregate cannot generally replace them. [Robins et al.](https://doi.org/10.1097/00001648-200009000-00011)
- Competing events are outcomes with event type, not ordinary loss-to-follow-up censoring. Cause-specific hazard removes competing-event cases from the subsequent risk set; subdistribution hazard retains them in its constructed risk set. [Young et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8471)
- `observed_until` represents an observed follow-up limit, but not why follow-up ended, nor enough to identify informative censoring or competing-risk estimands.
- Terrine's anchors, windows, aggregates, as-of attributes, and expansion cover the shape of basic sequential and fixed-treatment survival analyses; they do not alone express cloning, period-by-period treatment/covariate histories, or distinct competing-event and censoring processes.

## Methods notes

### Re-entry and person-trial identity

Danaei et al. explicitly repeated eligibility assessment at monthly trial baselines. Eligibility could recur for people not yet treated; a person could contribute to many trials, and their baseline covariates were the most recently recorded values by each trial's start. This requires anchor-specific state, not a single cohort membership or a one-row-per-person dataset. Each trial's outcomes and censoring are measured from its own time zero. [Danaei et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0962280211403603)

### Time-varying treatment and per-protocol analysis

A per-protocol comparison that censors at deviations creates selection: in Danaei et al.'s example, initiators are censored upon stopping and noninitiators upon starting. They note that this censoring may be informative and that baseline adjustment is not enough; time-varying IPCW is used to adjust selection. Their weights require treatment probabilities at each time conditional on baseline and time-varying confounders; their as-treated MSM similarly uses treatment history. Thus the natural source frame is longitudinal, ordered person-time, not just a baseline feature vector or post hoc total-duration aggregate. [Danaei et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0962280211403603) The foundational Robins–Hernán–Brumback paper develops marginal structural models specifically for time-varying treatment/confounding. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/00001648-200009000-00011)

### Competing events are not generic censoring

Young et al. distinguish the risk of the event of interest when competing events remain possible (cause-specific cumulative incidence/crude risk) from the risk under a hypothetical elimination of competing events (net risk). Their observed data structure contains separate interval indicators for event of interest and competing event; after a competing event, future event-of-interest status is deterministically zero. The cause-specific hazard conditions on not having had either event; the subdistribution hazard keeps those with a prior competing event in a constructed risk set. Contrasts of hazards generally are not causal effects, regardless of the competing-event coding; define the estimand before selecting a hazard model. [Young et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8471)

### Censoring, follow-up, and time zero

Danaei et al. end follow-up at the earliest of event, death, loss to follow-up, or administrative end, and their per-protocol analysis adds artificial censoring at treatment deviation. These are analytically distinct endpoints: an endpoint time alone says when observation stopped, but not whether the participant had the competing event, was lost, reached data end, or deviated from protocol. Informative loss/artificial censoring needs measured histories that predict continued observation and appropriate weighting; administrative censoring can be represented by a known source/study boundary when independent. [Danaei et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0962280211403603)

Immortal-time bias is prevented at design by synchronizing eligibility assessment, assignment/classification, and follow-up start. Hernán et al. show that specifying the target trial makes the time-zero mismatch visible; sequential trial emulation assigns controls a valid time zero rather than comparing future initiators from an earlier origin. [Hernán et al.](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000000535)

## Implications for terrine

- **Keep the core anchor key as `(unit_id, anchor_id, t0)` and make frame identity trial-specific.** `Schedule`/`EventAnchor(each)` already map naturally to recurrent eligibility and sequential trial person-trial rows; document cluster-by-unit variance as downstream inference metadata, not a reason to collapse anchors. [Danaei et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0962280211403603)
- **Borrow established names:** *sequential trials*, *person-trial*, *grace period*, *clone–censor–weight (CCW)*, *artificial censoring*, *IPCW*, *marginal structural model (MSM)*, *cause-specific hazard*, and *cumulative incidence function*. Names help users specify design decisions explicitly.
- **Treat `observed_until` as a boundary, not an event/censoring model.** Preserve event type and censoring reason independently; don't fill a competing event or unobserved post-exit interval with zero. `data_end`, loss to follow-up, administrative end, protocol deviation, and competing event have different meanings.
- **Add a minimal longitudinal lane rather than overloading `Aggregate` or `As-of`:** `Expand` at relative period plus `History`/`At(k)` values for treatment and time-varying covariates; output event-type, at-risk, and censoring statuses. This is needed for MSM/IPTW/IPCW and interval-specific outcomes.
- **Add cloning only as an explicit strategy expansion:** `Clone(strategy)` duplicates eligible anchor rows into strategy-compatible rows; a strategy adherence/censoring rule ends each clone when its observed path deviates; attach censoring weights. Do not emulate grace-period per-protocol effects by a single future-treatment flag on the baseline anchor.
- **Competing-risk support needs cause semantics, not just another outcome aggregate:** retain one first event's cause and time, with separate observation censoring, then derive cause-specific risk sets/cumulative incidence or subdistribution risk-set representations. Choose/report a causal estimand (e.g. total effect on crude cumulative incidence versus direct effect under elimination) rather than implying a cause-specific or subdistribution hazard ratio is automatically causal. [Young et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8471)
- **Avoid pretending the five primitives cover all causal analyses.** Point-in-time aggregates/as-ofs are appropriate for baseline adjustment and fixed treatment; period-level covariate/treatment history, strategy clones, censoring status, and cause-coded events are irreducible inputs for the edge cases above.
