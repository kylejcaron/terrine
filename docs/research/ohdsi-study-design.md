# OHDSI study-design vocabulary and lessons for terrine

## TL;DR

- OHDSI separates **event phenotype/cohort definition** from **analysis population and time-at-risk**. A cohort entry is not automatically an analysis time zero or the start of outcome follow-up. [Book, Cohorts](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/Cohorts.html) · [Book, Population-Level Estimation](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html)
- An OMOP observation period is an explicit interval where event capture is expected to be complete enough for absence to mean something; outside it, lack of an event is not evidence of no event. [OMOP CDM v5.4](https://ohdsi.github.io/CommonDataModel/cdm54.html)
- Re-entry and repeated treatment are first-class: one person may have multiple distinct cohort periods; `firstExposureOnly` and duplicate-subject policies decide whether to retain or collapse them for an analysis. [Book, Cohorts](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/Cohorts.html) · [CohortMethod args](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createGetDbCohortMethodDataArgs.html)
- Index-date-day features are an explicit estimand/design choice in FeatureExtraction: `endDays=0` includes index date; `endDays=-1` excludes it. Do not equate this to point-in-time-safe by default; index-day data may be post-treatment or the treatment itself. [FeatureExtraction settings](https://ohdsi.github.io/FeatureExtraction/reference/createCovariateSettings.html) · [Book, Population-Level Estimation](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html)
- New-user active-comparator design, prior-observation washout, pre-risk prior-outcome exclusion, and outcome risk windows are separate settings. They cannot be reduced to a single “cohort” flag. [Book, Population-Level Estimation](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html) · [CohortMethod args](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createCreateStudyPopulationArgs.html)
- OHDSI's standard is not “estimate then trust”: inspect attrition, overlap/equipoise, balance, power, and control-outcome performance; empirical calibration models systematic error but does not prove validity. [Book, Method Validity](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/MethodValidity.html)
- For terrine, borrow explicit **entry / qualification / exit / time-at-risk / censoring** vocabulary and preserve repeat anchors. Keep terrine's `[a,b)` window contract distinct from day-based, often inclusive OHDSI settings.

## 1. Cohort definitions are event-based periods, not just member lists

The Book of OHDSI defines a cohort as persons satisfying criteria for a duration. A person can be in multiple cohorts and can belong to the same cohort during multiple distinct periods, but cannot have duplicate memberships during the same period. Cohorts are designed to be reusable independently of the analytic question: the analytic study intersects target/comparator/outcome cohorts later. [Book, Cohorts §10.1](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/Cohorts.html#what-is-a-cohort)

Circe/ATLAS cohort logic has a useful decomposition:

1. **Entry event / initial event**: event domain, concept set, and attributes define candidate index dates (`cohort_start_date`). A first-event rule and observation/lookback requirements constrain which candidate dates qualify.
2. **Qualifying criteria**: temporal rules relative to that candidate index date filter entries. They may be grouped, and zero-occurrence conditions express exclusions. Attrition per inclusion rule makes the effect of each filter inspectable.
3. **Exit**: an explicit strategy yields `cohort_end_date`: observation-period end, fixed duration, end of a persistent event/era, or other censoring event. A new qualifying event after exit may create a later separate cohort period.

These are distinct from *cohort era collapse*: after qualifying entry events are identified, nearby starts can be collapsed into one episode. A persistence window (e.g. allowed gap between drug records when constructing a drug era) defines continuity of exposure; it is not the same thing as the post-entry collapse gap. [Book, Cohorts §§10.2, 10.7.5](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/Cohorts.html) · [Book cohort-definition appendix](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/CohortDefinitions.html) · [Capr cohort builder](https://ohdsi.github.io/Capr/articles/Using-Capr.html)
Entry and inclusion criteria use temporal windows relative to the candidate index date; definitions may require a minimum amount of continuous observation before and/or after it. “First event” is itself a criterion, not an automatic property of a cohort: ATLAS can select the earliest event or allow all qualifying events, with later distinct periods possible after exit. Keep entry selection separate from analysis-level `firstExposureOnly`. [Book, Cohorts §§10.2, 10.7.3–10.7.4](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/Cohorts.html) · [CohortMethod data args](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createGetDbCohortMethodDataArgs.html)


A cohort exit can also be event-censored (e.g. discontinue follow-up at a competing event or new exposure). The outcome/competing event used for censoring is a design choice, not an automatic implication of observing it. The Book's example defines time-at-risk from the day after initiation until treatment cessation and allows a 30-day gap between drug exposures; the design must say what an event means for follow-up. [Book, Population-Level Estimation §12.6](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html#designing-a-hypertension-study)

## 2. Observation time, time zero, and risk windows

OMOP `OBSERVATION_PERIOD` is the interval during which (1) clinical events are expected to be recorded and (2) absence of a record can be interpreted as absence of an event. Periods per person are discrete, non-overlapping and non-adjacent; event records can exist outside them, but absence outside them is not interpretable. Incidence/prevalence denominators should use active observation time. Minimal observation requirements avoid treating very short periods as informative. [OMOP CDM v5.4, OBSERVATION_PERIOD](https://ohdsi.github.io/CommonDataModel/cdm54.html#observation_period)

For a comparative **new-user active-comparator** cohort design, target and comparator are initiators of alternative treatments, with baseline characteristics captured before initiation. This helps align treatment assignment, covariate capture, and time zero; unexposed controls are harder to make comparable. The Book's example requires a year of continuous prior observation and uses a same-indication comparator. [Book, Population-Level Estimation §§12.1, 12.6](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html)

CohortMethod encodes inclusion and risk windows with separate arguments. In current docs, `washoutPeriod` (default 365 days) is in `createGetDbCohortMethodDataArgs`: minimum continuous observed time before index, often used both to calculate baseline covariates and support new-user status. `firstExposureOnly` controls retaining only the first exposure; `removeDuplicateSubjects` governs target/comparator overlap (`keep all`, `keep first`, `remove all`, or keep-first with truncation), and `restrictToCommonPeriod` restricts to dates when both exposures are observed. The data-args docs note some of these can be applied at `createStudyPopulation` instead. [CohortMethod data args](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createGetDbCohortMethodDataArgs.html)

`createCreateStudyPopulationArgs()` defines `riskWindowStart` relative to `startAnchor` and `riskWindowEnd` relative to `endAnchor`; each anchor is `"cohort start"` or `"cohort end"`. It separately configures `removeSubjectsWithPriorOutcome`, `priorOutcomeLookback`, minimum and maximum days at risk, and `censorAtNewRiskWindow` for preventing overlap when a person enters another risk window. A study end date also truncates risk windows. [CohortMethod population args](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createCreateStudyPopulationArgs.html) · [CohortMethod data args](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createGetDbCohortMethodDataArgs.html)

### Index-day covariates are an explicit convention, not an innocuous default

FeatureExtraction supports reusable event-domain covariate groups (condition/drug/procedure/device/measurement/observation/visit; event rows, eras, overlapping eras, values and counts), each with `AnyTimePrior`, `LongTerm`, `MediumTerm`, and `ShortTerm` variants where applicable. Default window starts are `longTermStartDays=-365`, `mediumTermStartDays=-180`, `shortTermStartDays=-30`, and `endDays=0`; setting `endDays=-1` ends capture on the previous day. Its temporal settings let users provide paired `temporalStartDays` / `temporalEndDays`; these use **inclusive integer-day** boundaries, with day 0 the index date and day -1 the prior day. `includedCovariateIds` filters generated covariates; `includedCovariateConceptIds` instead selects source concepts used to construct them. [FeatureExtraction standard settings](https://ohdsi.github.io/FeatureExtraction/reference/createCovariateSettings.html) · [temporal settings](https://ohdsi.github.io/FeatureExtraction/reference/createTemporalCovariateSettings.html)

OHDSI deliberately allows index day in many propensity-score covariates because diagnoses leading to treatment can be recorded that day, while requiring explicit exclusion of target/comparator treatment and directly related concepts from covariates. This is a context-specific modeling convention, not proof that all index-day information precedes treatment. For terrine's strict leakage validator, defaulting feature windows to end strictly before `t0` is safer; any same-day exception should be explicit, audited and unable to include treatment/post-treatment information. [Book, Population-Level Estimation §12.1.2](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html#variable-selection)

## 3. Re-entry, varying exposure, competing events, and censoring

- **Re-entry / repeated exposure:** Cohort tables can encode multiple non-overlapping episodes per person. `firstExposureOnly=TRUE` is an analysis policy, not an inherent cohort constraint. Duplicate-subject policy is separately needed when people appear in target and comparator arms; keeping all can double-count people/outcomes, while keep-first/truncate or remove-all changes the analytic population. [Book, Cohorts §10.1](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/Cohorts.html#what-is-a-cohort) · [CohortMethod data args](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createGetDbCohortMethodDataArgs.html) · [Book, Population-Level Estimation, study population](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html#study-population)
- **Time-varying exposure:** CohortMethod's common new-user comparison anchors exposure at cohort start and can define on-treatment time-at-risk through cohort end, with exposure eras built using persistence gaps. The Book also covers self-controlled designs that compare exposed and unexposed person-time; SCCS can model multiple exposures/time periods and time-varying covariates, but it assumes observation-period end is independent of outcome unless using an extension. These are different estimands/designs, not simply a time-varying treatment column in a conventional baseline comparison. [Book, Population-Level Estimation §§12.1, 12.5](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html)
- **Competing events and loss to follow-up:** The standard cohort workflow uses explicit end/censor rules and observation-period boundaries; study end can truncate risk. A person leaving observable time is censored rather than assumed event-free thereafter. What counts as a competing event (and whether it censors, competes, or is itself an outcome) must be specified for the estimand. SCCS requires care when outcome occurrence can cause observation to end (e.g. fatal events). [OMOP CDM v5.4](https://ohdsi.github.io/CommonDataModel/cdm54.html#observation_period) · [Book, Population-Level Estimation §12.5](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html#the-self-controlled-case-series-design) · [Book, Method Validity §18.1](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/MethodValidity.html#design-specific-diagnostics)
- **CohortIncidence:** Relevant for incidence rates/proportions and recurrent outcomes. Its design exposes multiple target periods, time-at-risk anchored on cohort start/end, observation-period truncation, first-at-risk and first-post-outcome options, and outcome clean windows. This makes repeat counting and recurrence-gap policy explicit. [CohortIncidence overview](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortIncidence/) · [method documentation](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortIncidence/articles/cohortincidence-method-documentation.html)

## 4. Diagnostics and validity are workflow gates

OHDSI's method-validity chapter frames validity as whether the method, data, and implementation support the question; it recommends standardized diagnostics on each analysis. For CohortMethod, outputs include propensity-score distributions/model inspection, covariate balance, attrition, power and Kaplan–Meier curves. These answer different questions: overlap, measured balance, selection/transportability, informativeness and time-to-event behavior. A standardized-difference rule such as <0.1 is a diagnostic convention, not proof of exchangeability. [Book, Method Validity §§18.1–18.3](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/MethodValidity.html) · [Book, Population-Level Estimation §12.1.5](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html#balance)

Negative controls should be plausible outcomes not caused or prevented by either exposure, observable with enough events, and chosen to probe a relevant range of biases. OHDSI recommends manual review of candidates and typically a large collection (the Book gives 50–100 as a rule of thumb). Empirical calibration uses negative-control estimates to estimate systematic-error distributions and calibrate p-values; positive controls (often synthesized from negatives) help calibrate confidence intervals and assess operating characteristics. Calibration quantifies evidence about residual error; it does not guarantee no unmeasured bias. [Book, Method Validity §§18.2–18.3](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/MethodValidity.html)
Current CohortMethod export diagnostics are configurable thresholds rather than universal pass criteria: defaults include maximum MDRR 10, maximum absolute standardized difference 0.1, minimum equipoise 0.2, and maximum expected absolute systematic error (EASE) 0.25. `computeEquipoise()` uses preference-score bounds 0.3–0.7 by default; MDRR estimates the detectable relative risk from observed sample size and outcome counts. These are workflow thresholds, not validated universal cutoffs. [CohortMethod diagnostic thresholds](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createCmDiagnosticThresholds.html) · [equipoise](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/computeEquipoise.html) · [MDRR](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/computeMdrr.html)


## 5. HADES / Strategus reproducibility

Strategus is an R package in HADES for specifying, executing and reviewing analysis specifications against OMOP CDM data using HADES modules. The design lesson for terrine is to make the serialized spec a complete analysis contract: explicit study population, windows, covariates, diagnostics, and execution metadata rather than relying on analyst memory or one-off scripts. Do not copy the R-specific orchestration/dependency model; terrine is planned as an ibis spec/compiler. [Strategus](https://ohdsi.github.io/Strategus/) · [Strategus introduction](https://ohdsi.github.io/Strategus/articles/IntroductionToStrategus.html)
The Book's ATLAS workflow packages study settings and includes a Shiny results/diagnostics app in the generated study R package; Strategus generalizes the executable analysis specification across HADES modules. For terrine, borrow the portable, reviewable study-package boundary while leaving the runtime/orchestration interchangeable. [Book, Population-Level Estimation §§12.7–12.8](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html) · [Book, Method Validity §18.3](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/MethodValidity.html)

## 6. Mapping OHDSI concepts to terrine

| OHDSI concept | terrine primitive / status | Mapping note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `OBSERVATION_PERIOD` and observed follow-up end | **Censoring property** (`observed_until`) | Intersect every outcome/risk window with observed period and data end; post-censor cells null, not zero. Multiple disjoint observation intervals require care; do not bridge gaps. |
| Entry event / index date / first or each event | **Anchor** (`EventAnchor`, `FixedDate`, `Schedule`) | `first`/`each` correspond directly. Distinguish entry selection from later trial enrollment and expose `nth` if desired. |
| Eligibility and inclusion / exclusion criteria | **Eligibility composition** over Aggregate/As-of + windows | OHDSI expresses exclusion via zero occurrences. Keep criteria relative to candidate anchor; retain attrition counts as explainable outputs. |
| Continuous observation / new-user washout | **Eligibility + Window** | Prior continuous observed duration is a distinct constraint from absence of prior treatment; encode both when needed, not one generic washout. |
| First exposure vs all exposures; repeated cohort episodes | **Anchor selection / repeated anchors** | Preserve all eligible entries by default where estimand permits; first-only is an analysis policy. `anchor_id` must preserve episode/trial identity. |
| Cohort exit strategy / end-date strategy | **Censoring property + risk Window** | Observation end, fixed-duration end, exposure-era end and censoring event should be explicit competing end rules. |
| Persistence window for drug eras | **Window / era-building semantic gap** | Not identical to `Window` for analysis; terrine may need semantic-layer event-era support or a named gap-tolerant continuity operation. |
| Era collapse gap | **Anchor deduplication / unmodeled policy** | Separate from exposure persistence; may merge close starts. Must preserve deterministic representative `t0` and explain discarded starts. |
| Outcome lookback / prior outcome exclusion | **Eligibility Aggregate before risk-window start** | Make lookback and event definition explicit; episodic vs chronic outcomes differ. |
| Risk window start/end, cohort-start/end anchors | **Window** | Translate to `[a,b)` only after documenting OHDSI's date inclusivity/day arithmetic; permit endpoint references to anchor start/end. |
| `minDaysAtRisk`, max risk duration | **Eligibility/censoring rule** | Reject/remove too-short follow-up or truncate maximum follow-up; record attrition/reason. |
| Target/comparator and common calendar period | **Treatment role + eligibility / Schedule** | Comparator is a real active treatment, and common-period restriction prevents calendar-time support mismatch. Sequential trials naturally encode repeated calendar cutoffs. |
| Covariate groups and temporal covariates | **Aggregate + role=feature + Window** | Keep event/era/value/count feature choices named; point-in-time validator constrains all feature windows, including grouped temporal windows. |
| Negative/positive controls; empirical calibration | **Diagnostics / estimate-validation gate** | Not ordinary study columns; store control hypotheses/results and calibration provenance beside estimates. |
| Equipoise, balance, attrition, MDRR/power, KM | **Diagnostics gate** | Separate overlap, balance, sample information, attrition and survival assumptions. Require user-configurable gate outcomes before promoting estimates. |
| SCCS | **Different design adapter / later** | Self-controlled person-time with time-varying exposure and time-varying confounding requires design/estimator-specific assumptions; do not imply a conventional anchor frame alone makes SCCS. |
| CohortIncidence recurrence clean window | **Outcome Aggregate + censoring/exclusion Window** | Recurrence gap, recurrent outcome count and first-event policy must be explicit. |

## 7. OHDSI lessons terrine should copy

1. **Time zero is a design decision.** Define one anchor per eligible unit/episode and align treatment assignment, pre-treatment covariates and outcome risk time to it. Don't give controls a later or immortal-time-free exposure-based t0 by construction; use a matched t0 or scheduled sequential trial when appropriate. [Book, Population-Level Estimation](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html)
2. **Washout is not one concept.** Distinguish (a) prior continuous observation sufficient to know history, (b) prior exposure-free interval for new-user status, and (c) prior-outcome lookback to define incident outcomes. OHDSI APIs separately model observation washout and prior outcome lookback. [CohortMethod data args](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createGetDbCohortMethodDataArgs.html) · [population args](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createCreateStudyPopulationArgs.html)
3. **Index-date-day leakage must be visible.** `endDays=0` includes day zero and `-1` excludes it, but including day zero safely requires knowing the ordering/semantics of records. Terrine SHOULD reject same-day features by default and require explicit exception plus treatment/event exclusion. [FeatureExtraction settings](https://ohdsi.github.io/FeatureExtraction/reference/createCovariateSettings.html) · [Book](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/PopulationLevelEstimation.html#variable-selection)
4. **Observation end is censoring, not a negative outcome.** Clamp observed follow-up at `OBSERVATION_PERIOD` end and make unsupported intervals null; assess whether censoring is outcome-dependent for designs such as SCCS. [OMOP CDM v5.4](https://ohdsi.github.io/CommonDataModel/cdm54.html#observation_period) · [Book, Method Validity](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/MethodValidity.html#design-specific-diagnostics)
5. **Repeated entry is not accidental duplication.** Keep distinct episodes/anchors where appropriate, cluster/repeated-unit inference as needed, and require an explicit first-only, keep-all, overlap truncation, or remove policy when arms/risk windows overlap. [Book, Cohorts](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/Cohorts.html) · [CohortMethod args](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createGetDbCohortMethodDataArgs.html)
6. **Diagnostics gate interpretation.** Report attrition, balance, propensity overlap/equipoise and power (including minimum detectable relative risk where meaningful), then assess negative-control calibration and study-specific assumptions before treating estimates as decision-grade. These are evidence about validity, not an automatic causal certificate. [Book, Method Validity](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/MethodValidity.html) · [CohortMethod diagnostics](https://ohdsi.github.io/CohortMethod/reference/createCmDiagnosticThresholds.html)
